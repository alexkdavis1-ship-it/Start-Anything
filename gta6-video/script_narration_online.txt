The biggest part of GTA Six isn't in the game you just downloaded. GTA Online turned GTA Five into one of the most profitable entertainment products ever made. And the version built for GTA Six still has no release date, no trailer, and no official name. So I went through everything that's out there and sorted it into three piles. Confirmed. Reported. And rumor. By the end, you'll know what to expect, and what to do while you wait.
Pile one. Confirmed. Number one. GTA Six launched as a single-player game. Both the PlayStation and Xbox store pages call it a single-player experience. There is no online mode in it today.
Number two. The current GTA Online isn't going anywhere. Take-Two's C.E.O., Strauss Zelnick, told investors, quote, we expect to continue to support GTA Online going forward. So your GTA Five character is safe for now.
Number three. Nothing transfers. Rockstar has not announced any way to move your character, your cash, or your cars into GTA Six. Until they say otherwise, assume you start from zero.
Number four. Rockstar owns the biggest role-play platform in gaming. In August 2023, it bought the team behind Five M, the mod that powers most GTA role-play servers. Role-play is what kept GTA Five at the top of Twitch for years. Rockstar didn't buy that to leave it on the shelf.
If you've never seen role-play, here's the short version. Players join a shared server and stay in character. Cops, paramedics, gang members, taxi drivers. Streamers turned it into a daily soap opera, and in 2019 it pushed a six-year-old game back to the top of Twitch. Today that only exists on PC, through mods. An official version on consoles would be new territory.
Number five. GTA Plus already exists. It's Rockstar's monthly subscription, and digital pre-orders of GTA Six came with a free month. That subscription was built around Online. So it's reasonable to expect it to carry over.
Pile two. Reported. These come from named sources, but not from Rockstar. First, the date. The C.E.O. of Twitch, Dan Clancy, told Bloomberg that streaming will see a big bump when GTA Six arrives, and an even bigger one when multiplayer launches next year. That means 2027. He runs the platform that works with Rockstar on launch plans, so he would know. But Rockstar has never confirmed it.
Second. A Bank of America analyst note reportedly narrows that to somewhere between March and June of 2027. That's an analyst's estimate. It is not a schedule.
Third. The groundwork is already on your console. When pre-loads went live, the tracker PlayStation Game Size reported that the game's files include support for joining sessions and launching multiplayer activities. So the foundation shipped inside the single-player game.
Fourth. Rockstar has reportedly been talking to creators. In 2025, Digiday reported that the studio held discussions with top Roblox and Fortnite creators about building custom experiences inside GTA Six. If that's accurate, the plan is bigger than heists and races. It's a platform where players build the content.
Fifth. The story mode has no microtransactions, according to coverage of Rockstar's Extended Look. Which tells you where the spending is meant to happen.
Pile three. Rumor. Treat all of this as unverified. In the middle of 2025, a leak claimed the mode is code-named GTA Six M.P. It claimed Rockstar is targeting sixty-four players per lobby, roughly double today's limit. And that it would like to reach ninety-six.
On a map about twice the size of GTA Five's, that makes sense. Thirty players spread across Leonida would feel empty. But making sense is not the same as being true.
The same leak described something it called fluid access. The idea is that you join friends and start activities without sitting through loading screens and invite menus. It also claimed the mode was further along than the original GTA Online was at the same stage. None of that has been backed up by a second source. So file it under maybe.
Now, here's the part most videos skip. You don't have to guess how Rockstar launches an online mode. They've done it twice. And both launches tell you something.
GTA Online opened on October first, 2013, two weeks after GTA Five. And it broke. Servers collapsed. Players couldn't get through the tutorial. Characters and progress vanished. Rockstar apologized and handed every player half a million in-game dollars.
Then came the wait for heists. They were shown in the very first trailer for the mode. They arrived in March 2015. Almost a year and a half later.
And look at what the mode became after that. Businesses. Nightclubs. Flying motorcycles. A private island heist you could finally run solo, which didn't arrive until 2020. The GTA Online people remember took seven years to build.
It also got expensive. Early on, a high-end apartment cost a few hundred thousand in-game dollars. Years later, a single flying bike was priced close to four million, and a gold-plated jet cost ten. Prices climbed with every update. So here's the lesson for GTA Six. The first months of a new online mode are usually the cheapest it will ever be.
Red Dead Online went the other way. It opened as a beta one month after Red Dead Redemption Two. And then it faded. In 2022, Rockstar said publicly that it was moving resources away from it, to focus on the next Grand Theft Auto.
One more precedent. In 2022, Rockstar started selling GTA Online on its own, separate from GTA Five, and gave it away free on PlayStation 5 for the first three months. So a standalone GTA Six Online, with some kind of free window, would not be a surprise.
So the pattern is clear. Rockstar ships the story first. The online mode arrives later, and it arrives thin. The best content shows up months or years in. A 2027 launch with a slow first year would be completely normal for them.
And the money explains the patience. GTA Online sells in-game cash through Shark Cards. By 2016, Rockstar had already made five hundred million dollars from them. And CNBC reports the mode still has close to ten million weekly players. My read is simple. You don't switch off a machine that still prints money until the new one is ready.
There's also a clue in GTA Six itself. Look at the systems. Police who track your description. Robberies you can start anywhere, with no mission marker. Fences, stolen goods, registered cars. Every one of those would work with other players in the world. I can't prove they were designed for online. But they'd fit it perfectly.
Here's what nobody knows yet. Whether it's included with the game or sold separately. Whether there's cross-play between PlayStation and Xbox. Whether it waits for the PC version, which also hasn't been announced. And what it's even called. If someone tells you they know, ask for the source.
So what should you actually do right now? Five things. One. Don't buy Shark Cards expecting them to carry over. Nothing has been promised. Two. Finish the story first. The original GTA Online reused the story's map and its characters, so knowing both gives you a head start.
Three. Learn the systems now. The wanted levels, the fences, the car registration. Whatever online becomes, it'll be built on the same rules. Four. If you took the free month of GTA Plus, check the renewal date, so you aren't billed for something you're not using. Five. Ignore any date that doesn't come from Rockstar. Fake announcement screenshots have been going around all year.
Here's my prediction, and it is only a prediction. A reveal early in 2027. A launch in the first half of the year. Bigger lobbies. And a creator platform that rolls out in stages. If I'm wrong, I'll be the first one to make that video.
One last thing to watch. When Rockstar does announce it, look at what they say about GTA Five's Online. If they give it an end date, the transfer question gets answered in the same breath.
